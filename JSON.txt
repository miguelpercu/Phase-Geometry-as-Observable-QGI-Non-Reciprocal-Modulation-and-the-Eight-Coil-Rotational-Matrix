Independently, the realization of magnetic-free non-reciprocity through parametric phase modulation reported by Estep, Sounas, Soric, and Alù, <em>Nature Physics</em> (2014) DOI: 10.1038/nphys3134, demonstrates that the relative temporal phase of spatially distributed resonators (120° for N=3) can generate synthetic angular momentum and modify collective dynamics.</p>\n\n<p><strong>These results do NOT validate the Universal Applied Time (UAT) or Unified Principle of Causality (UPC) frameworks.</strong> Both experiments were developed and interpreted within established physical frameworks and address specific experimental questions unrelated to the validation of UAT/UPC. Nevertheless, they provide robust experimental precedents for treating discrete phase geometry and temporal modulation as primary experimental variables.</p>\n\n<p>Within this context, this work analyzes the eight-coil rotational matrix developed under the UAT/UPC program as an exploratory macroscopic platform. The system implements a synthetic rotational configuration with discrete phase increment Δφ = 2π/8 = π/4 = 45°, where physical coil opposition is replaced by controlled phase relationships. For eight ideal equal-amplitude components, S₈ = Σ A e^{i(φ₀+kπ/4)} = 0. Any measured residual S_res ≠ 0 must therefore be decomposed into instrumental, phase, frequency, electronic, and potentially structural contributions.</p>\n\n<p>During development, a candidate residual R ≃ 0.2971 was identified under phase/frequency confrontation, alongside a central-coil RMS scale RMS₀ ≃ 0.707001 ≈ 1/√2.
